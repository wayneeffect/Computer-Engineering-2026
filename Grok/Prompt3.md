**Yes, it is quite rare** to be highly proficient or actively working *across* all these areas simultaneously at a deep, professional level. Computer engineering (CompE) offers one of the strongest foundational skill sets for spanning them, but the breadth required makes true polymath-level competence uncommon.

### Why It's Rare
- **Depth vs. Breadth Trade-off**: Tech careers increasingly reward **T-shaped professionals**—deep expertise in one or two core areas plus solid breadth across related ones. Mastering hardware-software integration (core to CompE) while also excelling in quantum control systems, AI hardware acceleration, cryptographic hardware, cloud-scale infrastructure, data pipelines, web frameworks, and cybersecurity policies is exceptionally demanding. Most people specialize because fields evolve rapidly and require ongoing, focused practice.
- **Time and Cognitive Load**: A CompE degree covers digital logic, computer architecture, embedded systems, OS, networking, and some software—providing natural bridges to *all* the areas you listed. However, quantum computing often needs physics/quantum info expertise (frequently PhD-level), advanced AI/ML requires strong math/stats, web dev emphasizes frameworks and user experience, and data science leans into domain knowledge. Few individuals sustain expert-level output across *everything*.
- **Industry Structure**: Most roles and teams are specialized. You might work on AI accelerators (hardware + ML), cloud security (cyber + cloud), or quantum-classical interfaces, but rarely rotate fluidly through all without career pivots or senior architect/consultant positions. Generalists exist more in smaller companies, startups, or systems/integration roles.

### Where CompE Gives a Strong Edge
CompE graduates are inherently more **versatile** than pure CS or EE grads because of the hardware-software blend. This makes it easier to:
- Move into **AI/ML hardware** (e.g., GPU/ASIC design, edge AI).
- Contribute to **cloud infrastructure** and virtualization.
- Work on **cybersecurity** at the silicon/root-of-trust level.
- Handle **IT/systems** with deeper performance knowledge.
- Transition into **software engineering** (especially backend, embedded, or performance-critical).
- Explore **data engineering** pipelines or **quantum** control/hybrid systems.

Many CompE professionals build **broad careers** over time through job changes, side projects, or advanced roles (e.g., systems architect, technical lead). "Multi-skilled" or "cross-functional" talent is increasingly valued in 2026, especially for AI/cloud/security integration.

### Realistic Profiles
- **Common**: Strong in 3–5 areas (e.g., CompE → embedded systems + AI acceleration + cloud deployment + cybersecurity).
- **Less Common but Valuable**: "T-shaped" with one deep specialty (quantum hardware, AI chips) and working knowledge of others.
- **Rare**: True expert/generalist comfortable leading projects in *all* simultaneously. These people often end up in research labs, big-tech architecture teams, consulting, or academia.

**Bottom line**: A computer engineering background makes you *less rare* and more adaptable than many peers—it’s one of the best launches for spanning these domains. But expecting (or claiming) high-level mastery across the full list is uncommon and usually signals breadth at the expense of elite depth in any single area. Employers generally prefer proven depth with transferable skills.

If you're building your own path, focus on core CompE strengths + targeted depth in 1–2 high-demand intersections (e.g., AI hardware + cybersecurity, or cloud systems + quantum interfaces).
